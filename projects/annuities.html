Reliable data for financial reporting

Mukesh Seerapu

Annuity policy, premium, withdrawal, and valuation data is the kind of data where "approximately right" isn't good enough — actuarial, finance, and investment teams all need numbers that tie back exactly to source systems. This data processing was running on on-prem infrastructure, and the goal was to modernize it onto AWS without losing that precision along the way.

What I built

Batch orchestration moved to Airflow, coordinating jobs across S3, Redshift, DynamoDB, and RDS/PostgreSQL. Streaming ingestion moved from Kafka to Kinesis — a deliberate trade, not a default choice — because it fit the AWS operating model better and reduced the operational complexity of running Kafka for a team not staffed to operate it. Large-scale transformations ran on Spark via EMR, with Lambda handling the lighter, event-driven processing that didn't need a full cluster.

Reconciliation as a first-class step

For actuarial and finance reporting, "the pipeline succeeded" isn't the same as "the numbers are right." I built reusable Redshift views and stored procedures for recurring reporting needs, with reconciliation that could prove the warehouse matched the source system down to the specific policy or transaction batch — not just a row count check, but something an auditor could actually follow.

The result

An AWS-native batch and streaming platform with reduced operational overhead, improved environment consistency, and reporting that finance and actuarial teams could rely on without a manual reconciliation step of their own.
